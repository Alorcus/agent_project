## Layer 1 — The project vision
 
**Grounding.** Every effort bigger than one human requires language and communication. As organizations grow — multiple offices, nations, time zones — direct contact with everyone becomes impossible, yet communication remains decisive for success. And communication is intrinsically hard: it runs on multiple levels at once (facts, emotions, goals, differing perceptions of the world). The project asks: **how can communication across stakeholders and humans be improved — and, in the broader vision, across humans and agents in an agentic age?**
 
**Mechanism: mine profiles from existing communication signals.** Companies already produce communication exhaust — emails, Slack messages, meeting notes. (Side idea: additional elicited signals like journaling or "grill-me" sessions for deeper individual understanding.) From these signals, build profiles of senders and receivers on two levels:
 
- *Psychological (individual):* what people talk about and why, what they care about, their values, goals, desires, fears, relationship dynamics, and fundamental drivers — the inner beliefs that shape many conversations (e.g., "I want to help others"). This layer is treated as essential, not decorative: structural facts alone do not make a profile that is recognizably a given person.
- *Sociological (organizational):* who talks with whom, which departments are close, which unspoken rules exist — mapping the behavior of the company as a whole through the communication of its employees.
**From profiles to a multi-agent system.** With a profile per employee, instantiate a multi-agent system where each agent represents one employee. The system's composition varies along two axes: *scope* (one person, a team, a department, the whole company, the company plus its context — competitors, regulators, customers) and *task* (what qualitative work the network does).
 
**Use-case ladder (increasing ambition):**
1. *Perception preview.* Send a planned decision/message into the agent network; receive each agent's response; get a feel for how the real organization would perceive it — before actually sending it. Also serves as the first fidelity test: how well does the network mimic the real participants?
2. *Digital twin as stand-in.* If forecasting proves good in some cases, the agent represents its employee when they are out of office or unable to respond.
3. *Representation in an agentic organization.* The agent is the human's face toward other agents, enabling humans to participate at agentic speed. The endgame: you can participate meaningfully in your company even if it is run by agents — but that requires an agent that carries your goals, desires, and perspective on the world.
**Profile fidelity as the moral core.** The profile should mimic the real human as closely as possible and be *in favor of* the human — carrying their flavor, how they perceive the world, what they value, why they matter in the company. This is what the network and its agents must capture. It is explicitly bigger than the thesis.
